# ADR 0021: Strata layout

## Status

Accepted (2026-11-10). Replaces the sizes, the growth rule and the satellites of
[ADR 0020](0020.strata-unit-layout.md); the rest of that ADR stands.

## Context

ADR 0020 laid the open panels out on units, with S 2, M 3 and L 5 units and
satellites: sections of an item that opened by themselves into spare units. The
layout work that follows it (sections that become columns of their own, several
views of one list side by side) needs two things that model does not have. A
section the user pulled out is a different kind of column from one that
navigation opened: it lives in the URL query, and goes back into its parent when
the user asks. And sizes need to be coarse: one, two or three
units, so that a column's size says what it holds and a screen has few cases.

## Decision

- **One panel for the route columns.** Trunk, branch, leaf and leaflet are one
  component, `EntityPanel`, with a `kind` (`infra/component/layout/entity-panel.tsx`):
  the header (title, chips, views, position, `⋯` menu, close), the content, the
  archive dialog and the stack of sections are the same, and the kind says what
  it has. A `trunk` is the first column of an app, a list: no close, no place in
  a list, no history, no archiving, no sections, its title is the place on the
  rail. A `branch` is an entity that is also a list (a count, a way to create, a
  toolbar of actions) and is always closable; a `leaf` and a `leaflet` (its side
  detail) are the entity or the page that makes one, and can handle form intents
  themselves (`intentHandlers`). The kind also decides the `DisplayType`, and
  so the size of the column, and the ids of the parts (`trunk-panel-content`,
  `leaf-panel-controls`, `branch-new-leaf-entity`) that the tests find them by.
- **Two kinds of column.** A **route column** comes from navigation: trunk,
  branch, leaf, leaflet, as before. A **split column** is a section or a view of
  a subject that is already on screen, pulled out into its own column. If it
  needs its own loader it is a route column; if not, it is a split column, a
  projection of its parent's data. Route columns live in the URL path and close
  with `×`; split columns will live in the URL query, as section filters
  ([ADR 0014](0014.section-filters-in-url.md)), and fold back into the parent
  with `⇤`. The engine knows both (`kind` on a column). A third kind, a **view**
  of a panel (see Views), is a column that belongs to a panel's subject but has
  nothing to fold back into.
- **Sizes are S 1, M 2 and B 3 units**, and a column may also be **FULL**, all
  `X` units, for pages that want the window (home, working memory). A unit is
  190px at most and `X = floor((width - rail) / 190)`, at most 9: 6 at 1280px,
  7 at 1440px and 9 at 1920px. Routes declare `strata: { size, grow }` on their
  `handle`. Without it a trunk, a branch and a tool page are B, and a leaf
  and a leaflet are M. Routes, and fallbacks, that were S, M and L in ADR 0020
  are now M, B and B with `grow`, which keeps them about as wide as they were at
  1440px. The trunks that stay S (docs, journals, metrics, smart lists, time
  plans) are marked `grow`, so a wide screen gives them room rather than leaving
  them a narrow strip.
- **Growth is one unit at a time.** Spare units go to columns marked `grow`,
  rightmost first, one unit each. A column on its own
  takes two when four or more are spare, as there is nothing beside it to leave room for. What is left is split in two, as empty slots
  to the left and right of the row, so the row sits in the middle of the window.
- **Satellites are gone.** Nothing opens by itself into spare units any more.
- **Folding.** The focused column always shows whole; others are added outward
  from it, the child first, while each fits whole; the first that does not is a
  peek and the rest are spines, as in ADR 0020. A split column is laid out like
  any other, and a view too: whole, a peek or a spine. None is ever put back
  into its parent for lack of room.
- **One header per column.** Every route column has one 36px header (`ColumnHeader`),
  made of fixed parts that each show only when they have something to show:
  `[title] [count | n/total ⌃⌄] [chips] ── [controls] [⋯] [× or ⇤]`. The title is the
  subject in mono capitals (amber while the column is focused): the name of the
  rail destination for a trunk (`Habits`), the kind and number for an entity
  (`Habit #373`), and where a page has no entity (a list, a page that makes one) the
  name its route gives its panel (`Questions`, `New habit`; the panel may
  say its own, as `Calendar exports` does), `Detail` only when it has none; a split column has `↳` and
  its section's name. Clicking the title scrolls the column to its top, and a click anywhere in a
  header (not on its close control) also selects the column, as moving to it
  with the keyboard does, so its count shows;
  the frame does it in the capture phase, so a chip that opens a view then
  moves the focus there. The
  count (the size of a list after its filters) is for the focused column, or the
  one alone on the screen, and is not rendered in the others. The position
  (below) and `⋯` are in every column, focused or not, so a mouse user can reach
  them without selecting it first: the others keep them with the title, the chips
  and the close. The chips say how the entity is shared: `● published` when it
  is published, `shared` when someone else owns it and it was shared with the
  viewer, and none when it is private; each is a button that opens the view
  that says more (Publish, or Access). They show in every
  column, focused or not. Close is `×`
  for a route column (it follows `returnLocation`, keeping the filters), `⇤`
  for a split column; a trunk has none, as it is always there. There are no
  buttons to widen or shrink a column, to scroll it, or to go back: the layout
  sets the width, the title and `Home`/`End` scroll, and `Escape` and the
  browser's back go back; and, on a big screen, none for history, publishing or
  access, which are in the `⋯` menu (below). What else a panel offers (filters,
  grouping, extra create buttons) is in the header too, as its `controls` (there is no `+` by default: each
  panel offers its own way to add an item, a `New` among its controls, first, so it
  is the one that stays when they fold), between
  the chips and the actions, left-aligned next to the title: drawn small (a button or a select is `controlSmall`
  tall), without floating labels. They are spread out as far as the column's width
  allows: when it is too narrow for them all, the last ones fold, one by one, into
  a `⚡` button, which opens them in a dialog at their usual size (`BarControls`
  measures the room and says how many are folded through `StrataBarContext`;
  they unfold one by one when it is wide enough again). A leaf has controls too (`actions`
  of its `EntityPanel`), as the settings button of a doc is; a button there that
  saves a form of the body names it (`ActionSingle`'s `form`, the id of the
  `SectionCard`'s form), as the header is a form of its own. A choice of one among a
  few (the calendar's period and view) is a filter, not a row of buttons, so it
  is one command, named for the choice, in the menu of commands (`x`). Tool pages have no controls and so
  no header.
- **The `⋯` menu.** The commands of a column, never things to look at. A leaf or
  a branch of an entity always has it, whatever the panel offers, with the same
  entries in the same order: "History", "Publish", "Access", then, set apart and
  in the danger colour, "Archive…" and "Delete permanently…". An entry the panel
  does not offer, or that does not apply, is disabled and not left out: archiving
  once archived, deleting until archived, and either while the entity can't be
  edited (`entityArchiveEntries`). Both open the same dialog as before, which
  asks to archive or to remove by whether the entity is archived. History,
  Publish and Access open their view as a column. `.` opens the menu of the focused column (`strataOpenColumnMenu`, which
  clicks the button marked `data-strata-menu`); `Escape` closes the menu first,
  and only then the column. The archive item keeps the id `leaf-entity-archive`
  (`branch-entity-archive`), the delete item is `leaf-entity-remove`, the view
  items are `leaf-entity-history-entry` and so on (a page with no columns keeps
  buttons, with the id without `-entry`), and the dialog's confirm button is `leaf-entity-archive-confirm`
  for both, so tests open the menu first (`click_entity_archive` and
  `click_entity_remove` in `itests/helpers.py`).
- **The top bar** has the path, whose parts are buttons that focus the column
  they belong to, and a map of the open columns: one square each, wide when
  shown, striped for a peek, thin for a spine, amber when focused, each a button
  that focuses its column.
- **The path is made of labels the routes supply.** A route says what it is
  called in the `label` of its `handle`, next to its `displayType` and
  `strata`: a string for a page that is always the same (`"New habit"`,
  `"Settings"`), or a function of the loader data, the path and the params for
  one that is an entity. `strataTrunkLabel` gives a list the name the rail
  gives its app (`Habits`, so the app and its list are one part, not two);
  `strataEntityLabel("Habit")` gives an item its name, found among the loader's
  values as the entity whose `ref_id` is the last part of the URL (and
  `Habit 373` for one with none). That is the helper, not a prop of
  `EntityPanel`, because a panel knows the entity's type and id but not its
  name, and the loaders' shapes differ. `strataCrumbs` (pure, with tests) turns
  the matches into crumbs: one for each route with a label, each owned by the
  first column at or after it, so a click focuses it; the last is the current
  page (`aria-current="page"`), and a long one is cut with an ellipsis, with
  the whole label in its title. The phone's title is the last label, and the
  part before it the one that came before. The URL is no longer read for
  names: a route that is a column and has no label fails a test
  (`strata-route-labels.test.ts`).
- **Sections.** The sections of one subject, one under another, are a stack
  (`StrataSectionStack`, with the panel id of its column). Each is a
  `StrataSection`: a 30px header with a `▾`/`▸` toggle, the title in mono
  capitals, a short summary and the section's own actions, over a body. The
  actions are small (`controlSmall` tall) and fold into the `⚡` button, the last first, as the
  controls of a column's header do, when the header is too narrow for them. A
  collapsed section is only its header, and keeps its summary. The body stays
  mounted, hidden, so a form in it keeps what was typed and still submits. Which
  sections are collapsed is a section filter of the panel (ADR 0014),
  `panel-id[collapsed]=section-id`, so it survives a reload and links carry it.
  A section can also start collapsed (`startsCollapsed`), and then the filter
  holds the opposite: `panel-id[expanded]=section-id` for one that was opened.
  Either list keeps an id only while it differs from how the section starts
  (`strata-section-collapse.ts`), so a link to a page as it opens has no filter.
  A section can be `lazy`, which draws its body only from the first time it is
  open or split out, and keeps it after, so what it loads is loaded when it is
  wanted and not with the page; and `bare`, when its body has forms of its own,
  as a form cannot be in a form.
  `z` collapses or expands the section the keyboard focus is in, and only that
  one: with the focus on the fixed first section, or on none, it does nothing,
  it does not go and collapse another; it stays out of fields, dialogs and
  menus like the other keys. A section outside a stack, such as in a form, has
  the header without the toggle. `SectionCard` stays for forms and pages that
  are one card.
- **The first section of a stack is fixed.** It is the subject's own properties,
  always there: it can't be collapsed and it can't be split out (`fixed`).
- **Split columns.** A section can be split out when it holds something worth a
  column of its own (`items` greater than 0): a list with items in it, or a
  single thing such as a note. A section with nothing in it, or which gains
  nothing from a column, such as a calendar or a summary, has no `⇥`. Its column
  is M unless it says otherwise with a `split` (a size, whether it grows, and
  optionally the size its parent has without it).
  It gets a `⇥` in its header,
  and `c` does the same for the section the keyboard focus is in, and for no
  other: on one that cannot be split out, it does nothing. Asking for a
  split adds `panel-id[split]=section-id` to the URL, in the order they were
  opened, and moves the focus to the new column. The column goes right after
  the route column the section is in. It has one header, with the section's
  name, the two buttons to its top and bottom, and a `⇤` that folds it back
  (also `⇧c`, and `Escape` while it is focused), last in the header at its right
  end as the close control is in the other columns; the section's actions stay
  at the top of its body. The section draws
  its body into the column from where it is, through a portal, so its data,
  forms and fetchers stay as they are, and leaves a placeholder row ("→ note ·
  in its own column") that focuses the column. Moving between inline and a
  column remounts the body, so what was typed and not saved is lost.
- **No folding back for lack of room.** A split column stays a column however
  little room there is: whole, a peek of what is left, or a spine, as the route
  columns around it. Moving the focus only moves and resizes the columns, it
  never changes which exist. An earlier version put a split back into its
  section, collapsed and tagged, when it would have been a peek or a spine,
  farthest from the focus first. That was dropped because the section's body is
  built again in its new place, which for a long list (the activities of a time
  plan has about two thousand nodes) froze the page for 300 to 500ms on every
  move of the focus across the line, and because the plan, the split's
  neighbour, then showed as the peek in its place. Only the user folds a split
  back, with `⇤` or `⇧c`. A parent's size still follows its splits (the time
  plan is B with activities inline, M with it split out). Phones have no
  splits: sections stay inline.
- **History and Sharing.** What a panel shows about its entity apart from the
  entity (its history, what was published, who has access) is only in its `⋯`
  menu, never a section of the panel and never a button in its header on a big
  screen. Each is a view, a column of its own (`useEntityViews`), opened from
  the entry of the same name, which becomes `Close …` while it is open. The
  chips of the header (`● published`, `shared`) open the view that says more
  about them: Publish, and Access (`strata-sharing-state.ts`). The chip for
  `shared` only knows that the entity was shared with the viewer: the loaders
  give the access status of the viewer, not the list of those it was shared
  with. A view opens right after the
  panel's own column, after those opened before it, and moves the focus there.
  The entry again, the `×` at the right end of the column's header (the same
  button as the close of a branch or a leaf), or `Escape` while the column is
  focused closes it and gives the focus back to the panel. The open views are
  kept in the URL as the splits of sections are, as
  `leaf-views[split]=leaf-history`: the panel id is `trunk-views`,
  `branch-views`, `leaf-views` or `leaflet-views`, and the split id names the
  panel and the view, as split ids are unique across the workspace. So a reload
  and a link keep them, a link back to the item from its list does too, and a
  move to another item closes them. They are independent: history can be open
  beside access. The body is drawn into the column from the panel, through a
  portal, as for sections. A view is not a section, so it has no place in the
  panel to go back into. Every view is M. A phone has no columns, so there the
  header keeps its three buttons (`#leaf-entity-history`, `#leaf-entity-publish`,
  `#leaf-entity-access`) and a view stands in place of the panel's content, one
  at a time, as it always did.
- **Position.** A leaf or a branch that is an item of a list says where it is in
  it: `12/63` in its header, with `⌃` and `⌄`, while it is focused. The list is
  not registered by the column that shows it: the item reads the page, as the
  footer does, and finds the items of the column before it in the layout (the
  ones marked `data-strata-item`, in the order they are drawn, after the filters
  and sort the page applied), takes those that have a link, and counts the ones
  under the same parent path as this item, so a list that mixes kinds (the habits
  among the inbox tasks of a time plan) counts only its own (`strataPosition`).
  The place is hidden when the item is not in any list there: opened from a
  link or search, or of a list the page does not show. `⌃`, `⌄`, and `o` and `p`
  (matched on `event.code`, with no modifier, and out of fields like the other
  keys) replace the open item with the one before or after it, keeping the query
  string, so what is split or collapsed stays, and the history keeps one entry,
  not one for each move. They stop at the ends and do not wrap. In the other
  direction, `j` and `k` in the list move the open item along with the selection,
  but only when an item of that list is open, and only to another item of it:
  with nothing open, they only select, and `↵` opens (`useStrataFollowItem`). On
  a phone, there are no columns to know the list from, so there is no position.
- **Subjects.** A route column and the split columns right after it are one
  subject. A 2px line along the top of every column marks it: neutral, amber at
  low strength for the columns of the focused subject, full amber for the
  focused column. The squares of the column map are grouped the same way, with
  a line under each group. Phones, which have no splits, have one column per
  subject.
- **Sizes by role.** Lists of things and small forms (time plans, docs,
  journals, metrics, smart lists, vacations and their items) are S; the forms
  that hold a lot (inbox task, big plan, calendar events) stay M, as at about
  205px a form row does not fit.
- **Keys in a stack.** With the focus in the focused column, `j` and `k` move
  between the sections of the stack (the section itself takes the keyboard
  focus, marked by a line on its left) and `↵`, on a section that has the
  focus itself, goes inside it: it starts editing its note, if it has one, with
  the caret at the end of what is written, and else it opens the first item of
  its list. `↵` is the one key for going in,
  whether it is a link, a section or a note. Like the other keys, they stay out
  of fields, dialogs and menus.
- **Items.** `e` on a section that has the focus itself goes into its list, to
  the first item, which takes the keyboard focus, marked by a line on its left
  as a section is. Where there is no list but a note, or anything else that can
  be written in, of the section's own, `e` goes into that instead, with the
  caret at the end of what is written, as `↵` does on a section. It does so in a
  split column too, where the section has no focus of its own and `↵` does not
  reach it, and in a column with no sections. The footer says `e edit` there. Then `j` and `k` move between the items of that section, and
  stop at the ends (they do not go on to the next section), and `↵` opens the
  one with the focus: its link is followed. `Escape` goes back up to the
  section (or to the column, for an item outside a section) and keeps the
  column open; another `Escape` is the close key. In a split column, which has
  no section with a focus of its own, `j` goes in from the first item and `k`
  from the last. `↵` on a section that is not a note opens its first item.
  A column with no sections, such as a trunk, or a branch or a leaf that is a
  list (the items of a smart list), whose list is the column's own, is gone
  through the same way from the column (`useStrataColumnItemKeys`, which the
  trunk, the branch and the leaf panels all use, and which does nothing in a
  column that has a stack of sections):
  `e` goes in at the first item and `j` and `k` from the first and the last,
  wherever the focus is in the column, and `↵` on the column itself opens the
  first item. A column alone on the screen is not marked as focused (`data-focused`), but it is
  shown as selected (`data-alone`: the amber line and title; not for a `FULL` column, which
  fills the screen whether or not it is alone), and these
  keys are for it. An item that was opened keeps the browser's focus while the
  page it opened takes the layout's, in the next column, so `Escape` goes up
  from an item only in the focused column, and else closes that column.
  What is an item is marked by the list that draws it, with `data-strata-item`
  and `tabIndex={-1}`; `EntityCard` and the card of an inbox task do, and any
  other list opts in the same way. Only the items of the section itself count,
  not those of a section nested in it, and only the ones that are shown.
- **Leaving a field.** `Escape` in a field leaves it and puts the keyboard
  focus on the section it is in, or else the column, so the keys above work
  again; what was typed stays. A second `Escape` is the close key. A dialog, a
  menu or a list of suggestions keeps its own `Escape`. The order of `Escape`
  is: the map or hint mode, find, an item (back to its section), a field (back
  to its section), and then the close of the column.
- **Hints.** `s` puts a label of one to three letters on everything in the
  columns that can be pressed or typed in, and only there: the rail, the top
  bar and the footer have the keys and menus of their own (`q`, `x`, `n`,
  `⌘K`), so they are not labelled and do not take letters from the columns:
  links, buttons, fields, selects, toggles, tabs and menu items. Typing a label
  does it, as in Vimium: a field takes the keyboard focus, with the caret at
  the end of what is in it, a select or a date opens its picker, and a button
  or a link is pressed, mouse down, up and click, so a menu that opens on the
  press opens. The labels are all of the same length, made of the letters of
  the home row and those near it, and handed out in the order of the page, so
  neighbours share their first letter. Each column that has a label showing is
  named at the top, in the middle of what is seen of it, with the title its
  header has (`Habits`, `Habit #373`, `New habit` for a new item), so a label is read as the column it goes
  to; the names follow their columns and go away with the last label in them.
  What is disabled, hidden, collapsed or
  covered (by a dialog, a peek or a spine, or scrolled out of its column) is
  not offered; whether it is covered is found by asking what is on top of it.
  While the mode is on the labels take every key: `Backspace` takes a letter
  back, `Escape` leaves it (and does not close the column), and a shortcut with
  a modifier leaves it and goes on, so `⌘K` still opens the search. A click, or
  a move to another page, also ends it. The labels follow their elements as a
  column scrolls or slides. Like the other keys, `s` stays out of fields,
  dialogs and menus.
- **Map.** `a` jumps to any column or section, where `j` and `k` go to the
  next or the previous one: it draws the open columns side by side over a
  dimmed window, each a box with its name and the sections of its stack under
  it, as the sections are on the page, and a label of one or two letters on
  every box and section. The labels are
  the hint labels (the same alphabet, handed out in the order they are drawn,
  so a column and its sections share a first letter). Typing a label goes
  there: a column is focused, which brings it in whole if it was a peek or a
  spine, and a section takes the keyboard focus, scrolled to the top of its
  column, so `j` and `k` go on from it. A click on an entry does the same. The
  boxes are laid out like the top bar's column map and the lines along the top
  of the columns: amber for the focused column, dim amber for the columns of
  its subject, neutral for the rest; a peek or a spine says so. A split column
  is one box with no sections, since its one section is the column, and the
  section it was split from is not listed under its parent. A section that is
  not in a stack, and so can't take the focus, is not listed. A column with
  nothing to jump to, alone, does not open the map. While it is on, the labels
  take every key, as in hint mode: `Backspace` takes a letter back, `Escape`
  leaves it and a shortcut with a modifier leaves it and goes on. A click
  outside the map, or a move to another page, also ends it. The map and hint
  mode are not on at once: the map listens first, and hint mode's own `s` is a
  label while the map is on, and the map's `a` a label while hint mode is on.
  Like the other keys, it stays out of fields, dialogs and menus. A column that
  is still coming in is waited for, a frame at a time, before the section takes
  the focus. The columns' names here are their roles (list, detail), not the
  subjects' titles that their headers have.
- **Find.** `f` opens a field at the bottom left, with the query and how many
  matches there are, and nothing about its keys: the footer says those. What is typed
  marks every place it is in the text of the open columns, whatever the case,
  and the one the search is at stands out; the marks are CSS highlights, so the
  page is not touched. Only the columns are searched, not the rail or the top
  bar, and not what is hidden, collapsed or folded to a spine; a phrase that
  runs from one element into the next is not found. `↓` and `↑` step through the
  matches while typing, going round. `↵` goes to the match and **the search is
  over**, the field and the marks gone: the match's column is focused, which
  brings a peek in whole, and the keyboard focus goes to the link or button it
  is in, so another `↵` follows it; a match in a note is gone into, with the
  caret at the start of the match, and then the keys type until `Escape`
  leaves it. `Escape` ends the search too, and puts the focus back where it
  was. There is no browsing of matches after the search: `f` starts again, with
  the last query typed in and selected. A click elsewhere, or a move to another
  page, also ends it. `Escape` is find's before it is the column's, and hint
  mode's before it is find's. Like the other keys, `f` stays out of fields,
  dialogs and menus.
- **Top and bottom.** Every column that has a body scrolls to its top with
  `Home` or `⇧k` and to its bottom with `End` or `⇧j`, in one jump (a list can be
  thousands of pixels long); a click on the column's title scrolls it to the
  top. This is so for route columns and split and view columns alike, and there
  are no buttons for it in the header. What scrolls is the part of the panel
  under the header, marked `data-strata-scroll` (`strataColumnScroller`), and
  only one that belongs to the column itself, not to a column nested in it. Like
  the other keys they stay out of fields, dialogs and menus, and they act on the
  focused column, or the only one.
- **Footer.** A 28px strip along the bottom of the window, on a big screen, says
  which keys work right now, in mono, a chip for each key and a word for what
  it does. It follows where the focus is, and lists a key only where it does
  something: `h` `l`
  with another column to go to; `j` `k` for the sections of the focused column,
  or for the items of its list when it has no sections (a trunk, or a list as a
  branch) or is a split;
  `↵` and `e` on a section, `e` only when its list has items (`items`) or a note to edit (`edit`, also on a split column or a column with no sections); `z` on a section
  that can be collapsed and `c` on one that can be split out, so not on the
  fixed one, nor with none focused; `⇧c` on a split column; on an item, `j` `k` between items,
  `↵` to open it and `Escape` to go back; and everywhere `s`, `a` (when there
  are two places to jump to), `f`, `⇧k` `⇧j` when the focused column holds more
  than fits, `.` when it has a menu, `o` `p` when the focused item has a place in a list, and
  `Escape` to close, when it can be closed (not a trunk). While hint mode, the
  map or find hold the keys it says how to use them and how to leave them, and
  in a field only that `Escape` leaves it; in a dialog or a menu, that `Escape`
  closes it. The rule is `strataFooterKeys`, a pure function of what the page
  shows (`StrataKeysContext`, read from the page by
  `strataKeysContextFromPage`), so it is the one place to change when a key is
  added: the footer lists what the rules above do, it does not decide it.
  What does not fit is dropped from the end, a whole entry at a time, so the
  keys easiest to do without come last. The page is read again when the focus
  moves and when its nodes or the attributes that show, hide or focus a column
  change, after a short pause and with a timer, not an animation frame, as a
  page that is not painted gets no frame. The columns, the rail and the debug
  overlay end above the footer (`--strata-footer-height`, which the footer sets
  when it is shown), and above the banner a build that is not the production
  one draws along the bottom (`--env-banner-height`), which sits under the
  footer. A phone has no footer.
- **The menu of apps and pages.** `q` (the key of the `APPS` action) opens a
  menu over the window that jumps within the app as the map jumps within the
  columns: it lists every place the left rail and the right of the app bar lead
  to, each with letters to type, and typing them goes there (`StrataAppMenu`).
  Three sections, side by side so that they fit, each after a rule: the **apps**
  the workspace has enabled, as the rail lists them (the same features gate
  them); the **support and shared pages**, as the rail's More menu groups them
  (Tools, Core, Process); and what the right of the app bar has (gamification
  for a user who has it, account, access, settings, and, off the app in another
  tab, community and help). `appMenuGroups` builds them as a pure function, and
  a destination is listed once, in the last place that has it, so the settings
  page is Settings and not also More Features. The letters come from the names
  (`strataAppMenuLabels`: `t` for Todos, `h` for Habits), every entry keeping
  the first letter of its name if nobody before it has it; they are one letter
  while there are 26 entries or fewer, and past that the entries that come first
  keep one and the rest take two, from a prefix reserved from the rarest
  letters, so no label is the start of another and a whole label goes at once.
  It works like the map: the labels take every key (`Backspace` takes a letter
  back, `Escape` leaves, so does a click outside, a move to another page, or a
  shortcut with a modifier), and the footer says so (`strataKeysContext` has
  `overlay: "apps"`). It stays out of fields, dialogs and menus, off a phone,
  and while hint mode, the map or find are on, and it holds the keys with
  `stopImmediatePropagation` so that those do not see a letter it takes. Logout
  and the terms and privacy links are left out, since a letter pressed by
  mistake should not sign anyone out.
- **The menu of new items.** `n` (the key of the `NEW` action) opens a menu, as
  the menu of apps does, that lists every kind of item that can be created and
  goes to the page that creates it (`StrataNewMenu`): by app, under the app's
  name, in the order of the rail, each with letters from its name
  (`strataAppMenuLabels` and the same keys inside it; `Escape` leaves). It
  lists the `new` pages that need nothing else open (a habit, a chore stack, a
  tag), not the ones that hang an item on another (a milestone of a big plan, a
  metric entry, a doc of a directory); an app whose features the workspace
  lacks is left out, as from the rail. The list is a catalog
  (`NEW_ENTITY_CATALOG`, filtered by the pure `newEntityGroups`) and not
  derived from the router, which has no list of what each page creates; a
  vitest reads the routes of the apps, whose ids follow their urls
  (`apps/habits/habits/new`), and fails when one that needs no parameter is not
  in the catalog, so a new `new` page is not forgotten. The footer says `n new`
  (`overlay: "new"` while open), and it is off in fields, dialogs and on a
  phone, like the others. The migration gives existing users `n`.
- **The menu of commands.** `x` (the key of the `COMMANDS` action) opens a menu
  over the window that runs the actions of the focused column by letter, as the
  menu of apps goes to pages (`StrataCommands`). It lists, under the title of
  the section each is in, the buttons of the section's header (create, add, and
  the rest), the navigations among them, and its **filters**, each one item
  that says what it has chosen (`Tags  Copii, Colinde`) and is marked `›`:
  typing its letters opens a panel beside the list with its choices, each with
  letters of its own, the one that is on marked `✓`, so a filter of many tags
  does not crowd the list. A choice of a few options sets it and leaves the
  menu. The panel of a filter of **many** choices is a multi-select with a
  search instead, as the selector on screen is, because with hundreds of tags
  letters and scrolling do not reach them: the keys write the search (any
  character; `strataCommandSearchKeyAction`), the choices that have all its
  words are shown, those starting with it first (`strataCommandSearch`), the
  arrows move the cursor, `Enter` turns the choice under it on or off (`☑`/`☐`),
  and the menu stays open and clears the search for the next. The menu is drawn
  again from the page after each (the sources tell the registry when they have
  been drawn, and an open menu asks again). `Backspace` on an empty label or
  search, and `Escape`, close the panel and go back to the list (`Escape` there
  leaves the menu), and the footer says how to search
  (`overlay: "commands-search"`); and, under the column's own name, its
  actions, such as `New`, which the panel's own controls bring; the commands of its `⋯` menu, and the way along the
  collection when the focused item has a place in a list, are rarely wanted, so
  they are kept apart from all of these, in a group of their own that is always
  the last, drawn as a column at the right (`strataCommandGroups`), whether the
  page has one column or many. In it `Previous item` and `Next item` come
  first (the arrows beside the item's count; the default keys `o` and `p` do the
  same without the menu, and `[` `]` were the keys before, which are hard to
  type on some keyboards; the migration moves a user still on them), then a
  rule, then the entries of the menu (History, Publish, Access), then another
  rule (the menu's own divider) before what destroys (Archive…, Delete
  permanently…). A command has `ruleBefore` where a rule is drawn above it. Nothing is
  written per route: a section's actions are drawn by `SectionActions` from
  descriptions (`ActionDesc`), and the same call hands them to the menu
  (`useStrataActionCommands`, a pure `strataCommandsOfActions` turning the
  descriptions into commands), so they are listed however the section draws
  them, including behind a drop-down, where the DOM has no buttons to read;
  `StrataSection` offers how it is laid out as the **last** commands under its
  title (`StrataCommandKind.LAYOUT`, which `strataCommandGroups` puts after the
  rest of the title's commands, set apart from them by a rule): `Collapse` or `Expand`, and `Split out` into a
  column of its own, or `Fold back` from that column. They are what `z`, `c`
  and `⇧c` do, listed only on a section that can, and a collapsed section,
  which has nothing else to show, is still there to be expanded.
  `ColumnHeader` does the same for the entries of its menu, and reads its main
  action off the page, as it is any element the panel puts there. A
  `StrataCommandsRegistry` (a context of the columns) keeps who is on screen,
  and is asked only when the menu opens, for the commands whose element belongs
  to the focused column itself, not to one nested in it, and is visible, so the
  actions of a collapsed section are not listed; they come in the order of the
  page. Running a command does what its button would: a navigation goes there
  with the filters of the other panels kept, an action submits the form it is
  in with its `intent` (through a submit button made for the moment when the
  real one is not on screen), a filter choice calls the section's own handler,
  so the URL keeps it (ADR 0014), and a menu entry calls its own. A command that
  cannot be run, as its button would be dimmed (the section is saving, or the
  filter choice is the one on), is listed without letters and cannot be typed.
  The letters are the menu of apps's (`strataAppMenuLabels`), given only to the
  commands that can be run, and the keys inside it are the same
  (`strataAppMenuKeyAction`); `Escape` leaves, and a column with nothing to run
  does not open it and leaves `x` to the page. The footer lists `x commands`
  only where the column has something to run (`strataKeysContext` has
  `commands`, read from the markers `data-strata-actions`, `data-strata-primary`
  and the menu's), and says how to use it while it is open (`overlay:
  "commands"`). It stays out of fields, dialogs and menus, off a phone, and
  while hint mode, the map, find or the menu of apps are on. Left out: nothing
  a section shows as a button; the controls that are not actions (a section's
  fold and split arrows, which have `z` and `c`).
- **The keys are the user's to change.** Every key above, but for the ones that
  belong to a mode or a field (`Escape` in a field, `↵` `↑` `↓` in find,
  `Backspace` and the letters in hint mode and the map), is the default of a
  **keyboard action** (`KeyboardAction`: column previous and next, next and
  previous, open, edit, collapse, split, fold back, hints, map, apps, commands, find, position
  previous and next, scroll top and bottom, menu, close). A user has a
  **`KeyBinding`** for each, in their web UI settings (`key_bindings`, a JSON
  column of `web_ui_settings`; the migration gives existing users the defaults,
  which are the keys that were hard-coded before). A binding is a key press as
  text: the modifiers held (`Ctrl+`, `Alt+`, `Meta+`, `Shift+`, in that order)
  and the key, which is the character it types (`j`, `.`, `[`, a letter in
  lowercase, `Shift+k` when shifted, `{` and not `Shift+[`) or its name
  (`Enter`, `Escape`, `ArrowDown`, `Space`). `strataKeyBindingOfEvent` is the
  one place that turns a key press into one, so what the user records is what
  matches later; with caps lock on a letter reads the same. Matching is on
  `event.key`, not on the key's place on the keyboard: the position keys, which
  were matched on the place, are now matched on `[` and `]` as typed. `Home` and
  `End` still scroll, whatever the user chose.

  The WebAPI keeps the bindings whole and checks them: one for every action,
  and no two actions on one key (`WebUiSettingsUpdateUseCase` refuses
  otherwise); `WebUiSettingsResetKeyBindingsUseCase` puts the defaults back. The
  defaults are written twice, in `default_key_bindings.py` and in
  `strata-key-defaults.ts` (for before the settings are known and for what they
  lack), and a test of the first checks that the second agrees. The root loader
  gives the bindings to the page (`useStrataKeyBindings`), and every handler
  takes them as an argument instead of looking at a letter: the pure functions
  in `infra/strata` (`strataStackKeyAction` and the others) and `strataFooterKeys`,
  so the footer draws the keys the user chose. They still stay out of fields,
  dialogs and menus, and what another handler took.

  They are changed in a **Keys** section of the account page, with every action
  listed under a heading and the key it has now. Clicking one waits for a key
  or a combination (`strataKeyCapture`; a modifier alone waits for the key that
  goes with it); `Escape` on its own leaves it as it was, so `Escape` for an
  action is set back by that action's **Default** button; **Reset to defaults**
  sets all of them. A key another action has is refused on the page, naming the
  action (`strataKeyConflict`), and by the WebAPI; to swap two keys, free one
  first. A choice is saved at once.
- **A split stays with its item.** Moving to another item of the same kind does
  not carry the open splits along; remembering them is left for later.
- **The engine** is `infra/strata/layout.ts`: pure functions with no React, as
  in ADR 0020. The phone layout is unchanged.

## Consequences

- At 1920px there are 9 units instead of 10, and a growing list is narrower
  than it was, as it can only take one spare unit.
- S, about 190px, is for narrow columns such as lists and trees of periods;
  routes with big forms stay M.
- The leaf expansion states, the satellites and `units.ts` are gone, and so are
  the expansion, shrink, scroll and back buttons of the headers
  (`column-scroll-buttons.tsx`).
- The header's count and `⋯` exist only in the focused column, so
  a test that needs them focuses the column first. A column alone on the screen
  is not marked as focused (`data-focused`), so the frame also knows `alone`.
- The history, sharing and publish buttons are gone from the header, on a phone
  too: they are `⋯` entries that open views. A
  test that opens Publish goes through `open_entity_publish_panel` in
  `itests/helpers.py`, which chooses the entry. `⇧k`/`⇧j` stay beside
  `Home`/`End`.
- The position counter reads the page, so it follows what is drawn (a list in
  another order, a filter) and needs no registry, but it also sees only what the
  page has drawn: an item of a list that is not on the page has no place.
- Sections can start collapsed (`startsCollapsed`, with an `expanded` filter),
  be drawn only once wanted (`lazy`) and hold forms of their own (`bare`). They
  were added for History and Sharing as sections, which were then dropped for
  the menu, so nothing uses them yet.
- Spread views and the keys that go with them (`v`, `1`-`9`, and `j`/`k` over a
  collection's selection), and views of a collection, are not built yet, and
  extend this model.
- Layout state in the URL (collapsed, split) survives an action: one that stays
  on its page returns instead of redirecting, and a redirect to the same page, a
  page under it or one above it gets the state added by a root middleware
  (ADR 0014). A redirect to an unrelated page, such as to the list after a
  delete in another place, drops it.
- Hint mode finds what can be pressed by its element and role, not by what it
  does when clicked. Something clickable that is a plain element with a click
  handler gets no label until it is a link or a button, or has a role.
- Find searches text, so what is typed in a field, a placeholder or a title
  attribute is not found; a column's key is on its frame
  (`data-strata-column-key`) so a match can bring its column into focus.
- A section id is its DOM id, so it must not repeat an id used elsewhere in the
  panel, such as the id of the section's actions.
- Item mode only knows the lists that mark their items. Tables, kanban boards
  and tree views are not marked yet, and a section with only those has no items:
  `↵` on it opens its first link, as before; a trunk with only those does
  nothing on `e`.
- A plan shown twice on one page, such as the current time plan above the list
  of all of them, needs a different DOM id each time (`current-time-plan-ID`),
  as a DOM id is one element's, and tests and links find a card by it.
