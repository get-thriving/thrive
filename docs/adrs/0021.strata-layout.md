# ADR 0021: Strata layout

## Status

Accepted (2026-11-10). Replaces the sizes, the growth rule and the satellites of
[ADR 0020](0020.strata-unit-layout.md); the rest of that ADR stands.

## Context

ADR 0020 laid the open panels out on units, with S 2, M 3 and L 5 units and
satellites: sections of an item that opened by themselves into spare units. The
layout work that follows it (sections that become columns of their own, several
views of one list side by side) needs two things that model does not have. A
section the user pulled out is a different kind of column from one that
navigation opened, and has to fold back into its parent when there is no room,
instead of becoming a spine. And sizes need to be coarse: one, two or three
units, so that a column's size says what it holds and a screen has few cases.

## Decision

- **Two kinds of column.** A **route column** comes from navigation: trunk,
  branch, leaf, leaflet, as before. A **split column** is a section or a view of
  a subject that is already on screen, pulled out into its own column. If it
  needs its own loader it is a route column; if not, it is a split column, a
  projection of its parent's data. Route columns live in the URL path and close
  with `×`; split columns will live in the URL query, as section filters
  ([ADR 0014](0014.section-filters-in-url.md)), and fold back into the parent
  with `⇤`. The engine knows both (`kind` on a column). Only route columns are
  built so far.
- **Sizes are S 1, M 2 and B 3 units**, and a column may also be **FULL**, all
  `X` units, for pages that want the window (home, working memory). A unit is
  190px at most and `X = floor((width - rail) / 190)`, at most 9: 6 at 1280px,
  7 at 1440px and 9 at 1920px. Routes declare `strata: { size, grow }` on their
  `handle`. Without it a trunk, a branch and a tool page are B, and a leaf
  and a leaflet are M. Routes, and fallbacks, that were S, M and L in ADR 0020
  are now M, B and B with `grow`, which keeps them about as wide as they were at
  1440px.
- **Growth is one unit at a time.** Spare units go to columns marked `grow`,
  rightmost first, one unit each. What is left is split in two, as empty slots
  to the left and right of the row, so the row sits in the middle of the window.
- **Satellites are gone.** Nothing opens by itself into spare units any more.
- **Folding.** The focused column always shows whole; others are added outward
  from it, the child first, while each fits whole; the first that does not is a
  peek and the rest are spines, as in ADR 0020. A split column that would be a
  spine or a peek is instead folded back into its parent, farthest from focus
  first and never the focused one, and the layout is run again, because a fold
  can change the parent's size. The caller gives the engine a `build` function
  from the active splits to the columns for that reason.
- **One header per column.** Every route column has one 36px header, with its
  name in mono capitals (amber while the column is focused) and the controls the
  panel offers after it. For now the name is the column's role (list, detail);
  it becomes the subject's title when the routes supply labels. Tool pages have
  no controls and so no header.
- **The top bar** has the path, whose parts are buttons that focus the column
  they belong to, and a map of the open columns: one square each, wide when
  shown, striped for a peek, thin for a spine, amber when focused, each a button
  that focuses its column.
- **Sections.** The sections of one subject, one under another, are a stack
  (`StrataSectionStack`, with the panel id of its column). Each is a
  `StrataSection`: a 30px header with a `▾`/`▸` toggle, the title in mono
  capitals, a short summary and the section's own actions, over a body. A
  collapsed section is only its header, and keeps its summary. The body stays
  mounted, hidden, so a form in it keeps what was typed and still submits. Which
  sections are collapsed is a section filter of the panel (ADR 0014),
  `panel-id[collapsed]=section-id`, so it survives a reload and links carry it.
  `z` collapses or expands the section the keyboard focus is in, or else the
  first section of the focused column; it stays out of fields, dialogs and
  menus like the other keys. A section outside a stack, such as in a form, has
  the header without the toggle. `SectionCard` stays for forms and pages that
  are one card.
- **The first section of a stack is fixed.** It is the subject's own properties,
  always there: it can't be collapsed and it can't be split out (`fixed`).
- **Split columns.** A section can be split out when it holds something worth a
  column of its own (`items` greater than 0): a list with items in it, or a
  single thing such as a note. A section with nothing in it, or which gains
  nothing from a column, such as a calendar or a summary, has no `⇥`. Its column
  is M unless it says otherwise with a `split` (a size, whether it grows, and
  optionally the size its parent has without it).
  It gets a `⇥` in its header,
  and `s` does the same for the section the keyboard focus is in. Asking for a
  split adds `panel-id[split]=section-id` to the URL, in the order they were
  opened, and moves the focus to the new column. The column goes right after
  the route column the section is in. It has one header, with the section's
  name and a `⇤` that folds it back (also `⇧s`, and `Escape` while it is
  focused); the section's actions stay at the top of its body. The section draws
  its body into the column from where it is, through a portal, so its data,
  forms and fetchers stay as they are, and leaves a placeholder row ("→ note ·
  in its own column") that focuses the column. Moving between inline and a
  column remounts the body, so what was typed and not saved is lost.
- **Folding back for lack of room.** `layoutColumns` folds back any split that
  would be a spine or a peek, farthest from the focus first and never the
  focused one, then lays out again, as a fold can change the parent's size
  (the time plan is B with activities inline, M with it split out). The split
  stays in the URL, and the section returns into its column **collapsed**, with
  the header, its summary and a "folded back · no room" tag, so the parent does
  not grow and push its neighbours out; the user may expand it. Its chip in the
  top bar is struck through. When there is room again, it is a column again.
  Phones have no splits: sections stay inline.
- **Subjects.** A route column and the split columns right after it are one
  subject. A 2px line along the top of every column marks it: neutral, amber at
  low strength for the columns of the focused subject, full amber for the
  focused column. The squares of the column map are grouped the same way, with
  a line under each group. Phones, which have no splits, have one column per
  subject.
- **Sizes by role.** Lists of things and small forms (time plans, docs,
  journals, metrics, smart lists, vacations and their items) are S; the forms
  that hold a lot (inbox task, big plan, calendar events) stay M, as at about
  205px a form row does not fit.
- **Keys in a stack.** With the focus in the focused column, `j` and `k` move
  between the sections of the stack (the section itself takes the keyboard
  focus, marked by a line on its left) and `↵`, on a section that has the
  focus itself, opens the first item of its list. Like the other keys, they stay
  out of fields, dialogs and menus.
- **Leaving a field.** `Escape` in a field leaves it and puts the keyboard
  focus on the section it is in, or else the column, so the keys above work
  again; what was typed stays. A second `Escape` is the close key. A dialog, a
  menu or a list of suggestions keeps its own `Escape`.
- **Hints.** `g` puts a label of one to three letters on everything on screen
  that can be pressed or typed in, in the columns, the rail and the top bar:
  links, buttons, fields, selects, toggles, tabs and menu items. Typing a label
  does it, as in Vimium: a field takes the keyboard focus, with the caret at
  the end of what is in it, a select or a date opens its picker, and a button
  or a link is pressed, mouse down, up and click, so a menu that opens on the
  press opens. The labels are all of the same length, made of the letters of
  the home row and those near it, and handed out in the order of the page, so
  neighbours share their first letter. What is disabled, hidden, collapsed or
  covered (by a dialog, a peek or a spine, or scrolled out of its column) is
  not offered; whether it is covered is found by asking what is on top of it.
  While the mode is on the labels take every key: `Backspace` takes a letter
  back, `Escape` leaves it (and does not close the column), and a shortcut with
  a modifier leaves it and goes on, so `⌘K` still opens the search. A click, or
  a move to another page, also ends it. The labels follow their elements as a
  column scrolls or slides. Like the other keys, `g` stays out of fields,
  dialogs and menus.
- **A split stays with its item.** Moving to another item of the same kind does
  not carry the open splits along; remembering them is left for later.
- **The engine** is `infra/strata/layout.ts`: pure functions with no React, as
  in ADR 0020. The phone layout is unchanged.

## Consequences

- At 1920px there are 9 units instead of 10, and a growing list is narrower
  than it was, as it can only take one spare unit.
- S, about 190px, is for narrow columns such as lists and trees of periods;
  routes with big forms stay M.
- The leaf expansion states, the satellites and `units.ts` are gone.
- Spread views and the keys that go with them (`v`, `1`-`9`, and `j`/`k` over a
  collection's selection), and views of a collection, are not built yet, and
  extend this model.
- Layout state in the URL (collapsed, split) survives an action: one that stays
  on its page returns instead of redirecting, and a redirect to the same page, a
  page under it or one above it gets the state added by a root middleware
  (ADR 0014). A redirect to an unrelated page, such as to the list after a
  delete in another place, drops it.
- Hint mode finds what can be pressed by its element and role, not by what it
  does when clicked. Something clickable that is a plain element with a click
  handler gets no label until it is a link or a button, or has a role.
- A section id is its DOM id, so it must not repeat an id used elsewhere in the
  panel, such as the id of the section's actions.
